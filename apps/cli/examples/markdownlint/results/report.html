Why it cost more

  • The baseline had a memory of its own: the repo's history. Before their first edit, agents without singularity looked up earlier commits in 8 of 12 sessions (git show --stat HEAD~1, then its diff) and copied the procedure for free. With singularity they did it in 6 of 12, after also reading the hand-over.
  • Hand-overs were often wrong for the task. They were picked by surface words: "documentation", "schema" and "built-in rules" put new-rule steps into the parameter task, the API export and the rename. Blanks were filled by position ({tag} = "# Install"). A one-rule spec written as bullets was read as four changes. Task 7 was told to use doc-build/, renamed away a task earlier. Agents ignored what didn't fit, and paid to read it.
  • Learning cost two thirds of the baseline's whole agent bill, and half of it (two rejected rounds, $1.51) changed nothing. The rounds took 2.5 to 14 minutes; the arm waited 41 minutes for them in all.
  • Where it helped: after the third round, the parameter task got one precise workflow (task 9, −14%), and the look-alike got only test steps (task 8, −32%).
